Advice

Advisory & Review

Prime contractors and their supply chains routinely invest months of business-development effort into a proposal, only to discover late in the process that it is incompatible with strategic priority, underestimates a sanctions or export-control exposure, or represents an unacceptable risk to a decision-making committee. Sinopia provides an independent, evidence-based read on a proposal, submission or board paper before it goes out the door — how it sits against current strategic guidance, where it's exposed, and what a sceptical reviewer inside government would ask first.

For clients with an ongoing government-facing program, this is also available as a standing monthly retainer: a short private briefing and a call, so leadership has an independent read on the shifting security environment relevant to your contracts, without carrying a full-time in-house strategist.

Research

Financial institutions are well equipped to answer one question — is this name on a list? Screening systems, list vendors and compliance programs all serve it well. They are far less equipped for the question underneath it: why is this list what it is, where is it heading, and what happens when it is wrong?

That second question is not a compliance question. It is a question about how designation regimes are made, contested, politicised and misused — grounded in specialist, peer-reviewed research into how these regimes actually operate. It is what we are for.

Designation integrity assessment

When a customer is caught by a foreign designation — a national terror list, a politically-directed listing, a contested international notice — the institutional default is to freeze and exit, because there is rarely anyone to ask whether the designation is sound. We provide an independent, evidence-based assessment of whether a specific listing is credible or politically motivated, written for a file, a financial crime committee, or onward reliance by counsel.

Financial crime teams · External counsel

Sanctions horizon and divergence briefings

Australian, US, UK and EU regimes are pulling apart in ways that create real exposure for anyone operating across them. We track where they are diverging, which designations are likely to come, and what allied policy shifts mean in practice — delivered as a standing quarterly briefing rather than assembled reactively from multiple advisers.

Sanctions · Geopolitical risk · Boards

Humanitarian carve-outs and restricted-jurisdiction payments

Payments that are legally permitted under humanitarian carve-outs are still routinely declined, because familiarity with the carve-outs remains thin and institutional risk appetite has not caught up with the law. We help institutions build a defensible position on what they can process and why — and help humanitarian and remittance organisations make that case to their banks.

Correspondent banking · Humanitarian organisations

De-risking review and defensibility

Exiting a category of customer is now a decision that must be explained — to regulators, to Treasury, and potentially to a parliamentary committee. We help institutions build the evidence base behind category-level decisions, so the reasoning holds up under scrutiny rather than resting on legal boilerplate.

Risk committees · Government affairs

The politics of designation — team briefings

Institutions train relentlessly on the mechanics of screening and almost never on the politics that generate the lists in the first place. A half- or full-day in-house session on how listing regimes are actually made, contested and misused — pitched at the people making the calls, not at a general audience.
